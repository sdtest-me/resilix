# Resilix User Guide
Version: 0.1 (Pilot)
Audience: HR Directors, COOs, Organizational Development Consultants
---
# 1. Purpose of This Guide
This guide explains how to interpret the Resilix interface during the pilot review process.
Resilix is a deterministic reasoning system designed to help organizations review observable operational patterns using:
- public ESG disclosures
- job-market signals
- governance communications
- evidence provenance
- traceable inference
The interface is intentionally structured to emphasize:
- transparency over automation
- observable patterns over hidden scoring
- deterministic reasoning over adaptive inference
- operational traceability over narrative authority
This guide explains:
- what each section represents
- how outputs are derived
- what the system can and cannot support
- how to interpret evidence coverage and limitations
- when calibration may be useful
---
# 2. Before You Begin
Before reviewing the interface, it is important to understand several core principles.
## Resilix Reviews Observable Organizational Patterns
Resilix does not attempt to determine:
- employee psychology
- organizational "truth"
- future certainty
- hidden intent
- executive capability
Instead, the system reviews observable organizational signals and organizes them into traceable operational patterns.
## Resilix Uses Deterministic Reasoning
Outputs are generated through:
- fixed rules
- documented thresholds
- structural mappings
- evidence provenance chains
The same input produces the same output.
No adaptive machine-learning logic or hidden optimization layer is used.
## Resilix Prioritizes Traceable Inference
Every derived output references:
- source evidence
- triggered_by relationships
- derived_from relationships
- inference depth constraints
This allows organizational stakeholders to review how conclusions were constructed.

# 4. Hero Summary Section
The hero section appears at the top of the interface.
Example:
- organization name
- observable dependency summary
- evidence framing
- primary call-to-action
## What You Are Seeing
The hero section summarizes:
- the dominant observable organizational pattern
- the operational concern surfaced by the review
- the current evidence coverage context
This section is intended to orient executive readers quickly.
It is NOT:
- a prediction
- a guarantee
- a final operational conclusion
## How to Interpret the Language
Phrases such as:
- "Observed dependency patterns"
- "Observable governance coordination gaps"
- "Evidence suggests"
- "Operational fragility indicators"
indicate that the system is surfacing structured observations from available evidence.
The wording intentionally avoids predictive certainty.
## Evidence Coverage Reminder
Most pilot reviews display:
`Evidence coverage: partial (public signals only)`
This means the system currently uses externally observable organizational signals and does not yet include:
- internal process maps
- operational KPIs
- internal survey evidence
- internal governance workflows

# 6. Value Gap Score
## What It Is
The Value Gap Score is a deterministic structural indicator derived from observable inconsistencies between:
- organizational messaging
- hiring patterns
- governance communication
- operational structure
- public resilience signals
The score represents observable organizational alignment tension.
It is NOT:
- a prediction
- a probability estimate
- a measure of organizational quality
- a psychometric score
## How It Is Derived
The score is generated using deterministic reasoning and fixed rule mappings.
Examples of contributing signals may include:
- rapid operational hiring expansion
- conflicting governance statements
- concentration of operational dependencies
- inconsistent public operational priorities
The same evidence set produces the same score.
## How to Interpret the Score
Higher scores indicate stronger observable structural tension between stated organizational priorities and observable operational patterns.
Lower scores indicate greater observable alignment consistency.
The score should be interpreted as:
- an organizational discussion signal
- a traceable structural indicator
- a review prompt for operational alignment
It should NOT be interpreted as:
- organizational failure
- future outcome certainty
- executive capability ranking
[TRANSLATOR NOTE: Avoid translating "Value Gap Score" into language implying financial valuation or psychological assessment. Preserve structural/governance framing.]

# 7. Key-Person Dependency
## What It Is
Key-Person Dependency represents observable operational concentration within organizational coordination structures.
It identifies areas where organizational continuity may rely heavily on a limited number of operational actors or undocumented processes.
It is based on:
- observable dependency concentration
- governance structure patterns
- operational communication signals
- process concentration indicators
## What It Is NOT
Key-Person Dependency is NOT:
- a behavioral evaluation
- a personality judgment
- an employee ranking system
- a leadership diagnosis
The system does not analyze individuals psychologically.
The analysis remains aggregate-only.
## Observable Dependency Concentration
Observable dependency concentration refers to structural signal patterns indicating that operational coordination may depend heavily on limited organizational pathways.
Examples may include:
- centralized approval patterns
- highly concentrated operational responsibilities
- limited process redundancy
- repeated governance bottlenecks
This is a structural organizational observation, not behavioral profiling.
## How to Use This Section
This section is most useful for:
- succession planning discussions
- operational continuity review
- governance resilience conversations
- process documentation prioritization
It should be interpreted as:
- an operational resilience indicator
- a structural coordination signal
- a traceable inference output

# 8. Spiral Dynamics Map
## What You Are Seeing
The Spiral Dynamics Map is presented as an interpretive overlay.
It organizes observable communication and governance patterns into a conceptual visualization framework.
The map is intended to support organizational interpretation and discussion.
It is NOT:
- a scientific measurement
- a psychometric assessment
- a diagnostic classification system
## vMEME Distribution
The displayed vMEME distribution represents relative interpretive emphasis derived from observable organizational signals.
Examples may include:
- governance communication style
- operational coordination framing
- hiring language patterns
- organizational value signaling
The distribution is conceptual and interpretive.
It is NOT objective truth.
## Colors and Percentages
Colors and percentages indicate relative emphasis within the interpretive overlay.
They should not be interpreted as:
- exact measurements
- psychological certainty
- validated scientific quantities
The visualization exists to organize observable patterns into a discussion framework.
## Why the Overlay Exists
The interpretive overlay helps organizations:
- discuss coordination styles
- review governance friction
- identify communication tension
- explore organizational alignment patterns
The overlay is intentionally separated from the core reasoning substrate.
Core reasoning functions operate correctly with:
`vMEME: null`

# 9. Evidence Provenance
## Why Evidence Provenance Matters
Evidence provenance explains where organizational observations originate.
Resilix prioritizes provenance over sophistication.
Every output should be reviewable through:
- observable evidence
- derivation chains
- deterministic mappings
- inference constraints
## Public Signals Used
Current pilot reviews may use:
- ESG disclosures
- hiring patterns
- public governance communications
- operational announcements
- publicly visible organizational structure indicators
## What "Partial Coverage" Means
`Evidence coverage: partial (public signals only)`
means:
- only externally observable evidence is currently available
- internal operational context may be missing
- organizational nuance may not be fully represented
This is an intentional epistemic limitation.
The system avoids pretending complete organizational visibility.
## Inference Depth: 2
Resilix currently enforces:
`Inference depth: 2`
This limits speculative reasoning expansion.
The system intentionally prevents:
- long inferential chains
- unsupported interpretation cascades
- narrative inflation
Inference depth constraints help maintain epistemic discipline.

- reduce operational fragility

- improve organizational traceability

These steps are best used as:

- operational context

# 11. Calibration Sessions
## When to Request Calibration
A calibration session may be useful when:
- public signals appear incomplete
- organizational nuance is missing
- operational context requires clarification
- internal stakeholders want improved evidence alignment
## What Calibration Does
Calibration allows organizations to align:
- external evidence
- internal operational evidence
- process maps
- survey aggregates
- governance workflows
- organizational KPIs
Calibration improves evidence alignment and interpretability.
It does NOT create hidden predictive capability.
## Aggregate-Only Constraints
Calibration remains aggregate-only.
The system prohibits:
- individual employee profiling
- behavioral classification
- personal psychological inference
Minimum aggregation thresholds apply before internal signals may be processed.

# 12. Troubleshooting & Common Questions
## "What if I do not understand a metric?"
Start by reviewing:
- evidence provenance
- associated organizational signals
- operational context references
If interpretation remains unclear:
- request clarification during the pilot review
- document confusion points in the feedback template
- identify which term or visualization created uncertainty
## "What if I disagree with an interpretation?"
Interpretations are intentionally traceable and reviewable.
Disagreement is expected and operationally useful.
Calibration sessions exist to:
- improve evidence alignment
- introduce internal context
- reduce observable ambiguity
## "What if the organization has context not visible publicly?"
This is a normal limitation of partial evidence coverage.
Public-signal reviews are intended as:
- starting points
- organizational discussion frameworks
- operational review prompts
They are not intended to replace internal operational understanding.
