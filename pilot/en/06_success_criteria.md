# Resilix Pilot Success Criteria
## Purpose of This Document
This document defines how the Resilix pilot will be evaluated before proceeding to the next implementation stage (#13 automation layer).
The goal of the pilot is not to validate predictive capability or automation scale. The goal is to determine whether enterprise users:
- understand the epistemic boundaries,
- trust the Evidence Provenance model,
- find the outputs operationally useful,
- and engage with calibration workflows responsibly.
Pilot evaluation therefore prioritizes:
- governance trust,
- interpretability,
- traceability,
- and operational clarity.
Success is measured through observable engagement and structured feedback signals, not volume metrics alone.
---
# 1. Trust Signals (Qualitative)
Trust signals indicate that pilot participants understand the platform as a deterministic, traceable organizational review system rather than as a predictive AI product.
## Strong Trust Indicators
Participants use language such as:
- "This reflects observable realities in our organization."
- "The limitations are transparent."
- "The provenance explanation is understandable."
- "I can see how the interpretation was assembled."
- "This feels governance-oriented rather than speculative."
Additional trust indicators include:
- referencing specific Evidence Provenance chains,
- discussing Observable dependency concentration in operational terms,
- or asking how internal evidence may improve Evidence coverage.
Users demonstrating trust typically:
- understand the distinction between public evidence and calibrated internal evidence,
- recognize the Interpretive Overlay as non-diagnostic,
- and correctly describe the outputs as traceable interpretations rather than predictions.
## Moderate Trust Indicators
Participants:
- understand the interface after clarification,
- ask operational rather than conceptual questions,
- or request examples of calibration workflows.
These users may still require onboarding support but show acceptable epistemic alignment.
## Weak Trust Indicators
Participants:
- repeatedly ask whether outputs are "accurate," "scientific," or "predictive,"
- focus on numerical authority instead of provenance,
- or expect deterministic certainty from interpretive outputs.
Weak trust signals do not automatically fail the pilot, but recurring patterns indicate onboarding or framing problems.
---
# 2. Confusion Signals (Qualitative)
Confusion signals identify where users misunderstand the platform's epistemic constraints or operational purpose.
## Critical Confusion Indicators
The following statements indicate major framing failure:
- "What disruption does this predict?"
- "Which employees are high risk?"
- "What is the probability this happens?"
- "Is this a psychological assessment tool?"
- "Are the Spiral percentages scientifically validated?"
These indicate users are interpreting Resilix as:
- predictive software,
- behavioral profiling infrastructure,
- or diagnostic AI.
Any recurring confusion of this type requires immediate review before scaling.
## Operational Confusion Indicators
The following indicate onboarding clarity issues:
- "What does this number mean?"
- "What does partial Evidence coverage mean?"
- "Why does inference depth: 2 matter?"
- "How are Operational Stabilization Steps generated?"
- "What is the difference between calibration and onboarding?"
These issues are acceptable during early pilot stages if:
- clarification resolves confusion quickly,
- and users subsequently demonstrate trust signals.
## Overlay Misinterpretation Indicators
Special attention should be paid to confusion involving the Interpretive Overlay.
Escalate if participants:
- interpret vMEME distributions as scientific measurements,
- treat colors as employee classifications,
- or describe Spiral Dynamics outputs as objective organizational truth.

# 3. Engagement Signals (Behavioral)
Engagement signals demonstrate that users perceive operational value without requiring exaggerated claims.
## Positive Engagement Indicators
Participants:
- request a calibration session,
- ask how internal operational evidence can improve alignment,
- share pilot materials internally,
- provide structured feedback through the feedback template,
- or request executive walkthrough sessions.
Additional engagement signals:
- referencing outputs during operational discussions,
- asking governance-oriented questions,
- or requesting clarification on provenance chains.
## Strong Engagement Indicators
High-value pilot engagement includes:
- scheduling internal stakeholder reviews,
- requesting namespace-isolated calibration workflows,
- mapping internal KPIs to existing evidence structures,
- or discussing Operational Stabilization Steps in planning contexts.
Engagement is considered especially strong when users independently explain:
- deterministic reasoning,
- traceable inference,
- or aggregate-only safeguards to colleagues.
---
# 4. Conversion Signals (Decision-Oriented)
Conversion signals determine whether the pilot supports progression toward controlled automation workflows (#13).
## Strong Conversion Indicators
Participants:
- commit to internal data integration discussions,
- schedule executive workshops,
- request sponsor calibration sessions,
- or reference Resilix outputs in planning documentation.
Additional strong signals include:
- governance teams reviewing calibration policies,
- operational leaders requesting evidence alignment support,
- or HR leadership requesting controlled rollout discussions.
## Weak Conversion Indicators
The following are insufficient alone:
- general curiosity,
- positive comments without engagement,
- or requests for unsupported predictive features.
Requests for:
- forecasting,
- employee scoring,
- or AI automation expansion
should be logged as misalignment signals rather than conversion success.
---
# 5. Pilot Thresholds for Proceeding to #13
Proceed to #13 only if the following thresholds are met:
## Required Thresholds
### Trust Threshold
- ≥70% of participants demonstrate trust signals rather than confusion signals.
### Engagement Threshold
- ≥50% of participants request:
  - calibration sessions,
  - internal evidence alignment,
  - or executive review discussions.
### Epistemic Threshold
- Zero critical objections related to:
  - predictive framing,
  - behavioral profiling,
  - AI misrepresentation,
  - or diagnostic interpretation claims.
### Governance Threshold
- No unresolved concerns regarding:
  - confidentiality,
  - namespace isolation,
  - aggregate-only enforcement,
  - or provenance traceability.
If thresholds are not met:
- pause automation work,
- review onboarding materials,
- revise interface framing,
- and conduct additional pilot interviews before scaling.

# 6. Documentation & Logging Guidance
## CRM / Project Tracker Logging
Each pilot interaction should be tagged using structured categories:
| Category | Example Tags |
|---|---|
| Trust | provenance_understood, governance_trust |
| Confusion | overlay_confusion, prediction_expectation |
| Engagement | calibration_request, executive_interest |
| Conversion | workshop_scheduled, internal_alignment |
| Risk | profiling_concern, ai_misinterpretation |
## Feedback Documentation Requirements
Each participant record should include:
- organization name,
- participant role,
- pilot date,
- Evidence coverage context,
- primary objections,
- trust signals observed,
- engagement actions taken,
- and follow-up recommendations.
## Review Authority Before #13
Before proceeding to #13:
- governance review,
- architecture review,
- and pilot feedback synthesis
must all confirm that the epistemic framing remains intact.
Approval should not be based solely on enthusiasm or feature requests.
The primary decision criterion is whether Resilix remains understandable, traceable, and governance-safe under real enterprise interaction conditions.
---
# 7. Final Decision Principle
The purpose of the pilot is not to prove that Resilix is "smart."
The purpose is to verify that organizations:
- understand the limitations,
- trust the provenance structure,
- and find the deterministic reasoning model operationally useful without requiring AI theater.
Scaling should occur only if governance clarity survives real enterprise usage.
