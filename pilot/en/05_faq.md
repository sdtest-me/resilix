# Resilix Pilot FAQ
## 1. Is this AI?
No. Resilix uses deterministic reasoning, rules-based mappings, and traceable inference.
The platform does not use machine learning models, neural networks, adaptive scoring systems, or probabilistic inference layers. Outputs are generated through documented rule execution paths that can be inspected and reviewed.
Every derived output references Evidence Provenance links, including:
- observable source signals,
- transformation steps,
- inference relationships,
- and derivation depth constraints.
This means users can review how a conclusion was assembled instead of relying on opaque black-box processing.
Resilix is designed as an evidence review system focused on organizational resilience patterns, not as an autonomous decision engine.
---
## 2. Does this profile employees?
No. Resilix operates under aggregate-only analysis constraints.
The platform does not:
- evaluate individuals,
- infer personality traits,
- generate behavioral profiles,
- or assess employee psychology.
Internal calibration workflows require minimum aggregation thresholds (n≥5) before signals are processed. Individual-level records are rejected by onboarding constraints.
Metrics such as Key-Person Dependency and Observable dependency concentration refer to structural operational patterns, not personal characteristics.
For example:
- concentration of governance communication,
- concentration of operational ownership,
- or concentration of escalation responsibility
may indicate organizational fragility patterns without making claims about any individual employee.
---
## 3. Is this predictive?
No. Resilix performs evidence review of observable patterns, not forecasting or probability estimation.
The platform does not:
- forecast future events,
- estimate disruption likelihood,
- or assign probabilistic confidence scores.
Instead, Resilix highlights:
- observable coordination gaps,
- dependency concentration,
- governance inconsistencies,
- and evidence alignment limitations.
Operational Stabilization Steps are intended to reduce fragility exposure based on currently observable evidence. They are not guarantees of future outcomes.
When evidence is incomplete, outputs explicitly indicate limited Evidence coverage or ambiguity conditions.
---
## 4. What data is used?
Resilix uses publicly observable organizational signals and optional internal calibration evidence.
Public evidence sources may include:
- ESG disclosures,
- sustainability reports,
- governance communications,
- hiring and job-market signals,
- operational announcements,
- leadership communication patterns,
- and publicly accessible organizational materials.
Internal evidence may optionally be included during a calibration session. Examples include:
- aggregated survey outputs,
- KPI summaries,
- process maps,
- operational workflows,
- or governance review artifacts.
Internal evidence is used only within confidential calibration boundaries and remains isolated through namespace separation controls.

## 5. How are outputs generated?
Outputs are generated through deterministic rules and traceable inference.
Each module performs constrained structural operations, including:
- evidence normalization,
- contradiction detection,
- dependency concentration review,
- evidence alignment checks,
- and interpretive overlay generation.
Every output references provenance links through:
- `triggered_by[]`,
- `derived_from[]`,
- evidence identifiers,
- and inference depth controls.
Inference depth: 2 limits how far derived reasoning can extend from original evidence. This reduces speculation accumulation and prevents long inference chains from appearing more authoritative than the underlying evidence supports.
Resilix prioritizes:
- traceability over sophistication,
- structure over semantics,
- and governance visibility over automation complexity.
---
## 6. What does Spiral Dynamics mean here?
In Resilix, Spiral Dynamics functions as an Interpretive Overlay (non-diagnostic).
It is used as a conceptual model for organizing observable patterns in:
- governance language,
- communication emphasis,
- operational coordination,
- and organizational framing.
The platform does not claim that:
- organizations possess objectively measurable "stages,"
- vMEME distributions are scientific measurements,
- or individuals can be classified psychologically.
Instead, the overlay provides a structured interpretive lens that may help users discuss organizational patterns in a shared conceptual vocabulary.
Colors and percentage distributions represent relative emphasis within observable evidence patterns, not absolute truth claims.
Core reasoning modules remain operational even when the interpretive overlay is disabled (`vMEME: null`).

## 7. How accurate is it?
Resilix does not position outputs as universally accurate or objectively complete.
Interpretation quality depends on Evidence coverage.
Examples:
- **Partial coverage** = public signals only
- **Expanded coverage** = public + calibrated internal evidence
Public evidence may reveal:
- governance concentration,
- hiring volatility,
- or communication inconsistencies,
while internal calibration may improve organizational alignment visibility through additional operational context.
Resilix therefore frames outputs as:
- observable indicators,
- traceable interpretations,
- and evidence-constrained organizational signals.
The platform intentionally avoids probabilistic confidence claims or certainty language.
---
## 8. What's the difference between public and internal data?
Public evidence consists of externally observable organizational signals, including:
- ESG disclosures,
- hiring activity,
- governance communications,
- public reports,
- and operational announcements.
Internal evidence refers to calibrated operational materials voluntarily provided during a calibration session. Examples include:
- aggregated surveys,
- KPI summaries,
- process documentation,
- governance workflows,
- or coordination maps.
Public evidence provides broad structural visibility.
Internal evidence may improve:
- evidence alignment,
- contradiction resolution,
- and operational context visibility.
Both evidence types remain subject to:
- aggregate-only constraints,
- provenance tracking,
- and deterministic processing rules.
---
## 9. Is my data private?
Yes. Internal calibration data is processed under confidential calibration controls.
Key safeguards include:
- namespace isolation,
- aggregate-only outputs,
- provenance tracking,
- and restricted calibration boundaries.
Resilix rejects:
- individual employee records,
- covert monitoring practices,
- and hidden behavioral inference workflows.
Calibration sessions are designed for organizational evidence alignment, not surveillance.
The platform architecture intentionally limits inference expansion through:
- deterministic rules,
- provenance_depth_limit controls,
- and explicit evidence lineage requirements.
[TRANSLATOR NOTE: RU/KZ audiences may expect stronger governance language around confidentiality and executive access controls.]
---
## 10. What if I disagree with an interpretation?
Disagreement is expected and operationally useful.
Resilix outputs are intentionally traceable so interpretations can be reviewed against their underlying evidence.
A calibration session allows:
- evidence alignment,
- contradiction clarification,
- contextual adjustment,
- and provenance review using internal operational evidence.
Because the system is deterministic and provenance-first, users can inspect:
- which signals contributed,
- which rules executed,
- and which evidence relationships produced a result.
Interpretations are therefore adjustable through better evidence alignment rather than hidden model retraining or opaque scoring changes.
This approach supports governance transparency and collaborative organizational review workflows.
